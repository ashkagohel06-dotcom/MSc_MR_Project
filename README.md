# Introduction
Heart failure (HF) remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, yet the biological mechanisms that drive the transition from subclinical ventricular remodelling to overt clinical disease remain poorly understood. Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging provides gold-standard measures of ventricular structure and function, including left ventricular (LV) mass, volumes, and ejection fraction, as well as right ventricular (RV) morphology and performance. Large-scale genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have recently identified genetic variants influencing these CMR-derived phenotypes, offering an opportunity to interrogate their causal role in HF development. Recent HF GWAS have also grown in size, providing genome-wide association analyses stratified by all-cause HF, HFrEF (Heart Failure with reduced Ejection Fraction) and HFpEF (Heart Failure with preserved Ejection Fraction)1. Mendelian Randomisation (MR) is a genetic epidemiological framework that uses inherited genetic variants as instrumental variables to infer causal relationships between exposures and outcomes. Recent work, including a Mendelian randomisation analysis of cardiac MRI measurements as surrogate outcomes for heart failure and atrial fibrillation2, suggests that ventricular ejection fraction and systolic volumes may act as genetic surrogates for future HF risk. However, whether distinct patterns of ventricular remodelling differentially contribute to HF subtypes (e.g., preserved vs reduced ejection fraction) remains unclear, particularly for HFpEF, which is biologically heterogeneous, therapeutically challenging, and substantially less well understood than HFrEF.
